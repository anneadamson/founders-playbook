## 3. Making decisions

**Who sits on the management board (the people who run the company day to day and can sign for it)?**

*Why does it matter?* In an Estonian OÜ the management board represents the company. Each board member can usually sign alone, which means each can bind the company by themselves. Fewer board members means fewer people who can sign by mistake.
- ★ All founders
- One founder only

**If one founder only: who?**

*Why does it matter?* That founder runs daily operations. The others keep control through the big decisions below.

**How strong must a vote be to decide the big things?**

*Why does it matter?* With two founders at 50/50, "more than half" can never be reached unless both agree, so you are in a deadlock whenever you disagree. With three equal founders, two-thirds lets any two outvote the third. Three-quarters or "everyone" gives each founder a veto. The law itself requires at least two-thirds to change the articles (Commercial Code § 175).
- More than half of the votes
- ★ At least two-thirds of the votes
- At least three-quarters of the votes
- Everyone must agree

**Above what amount (in euros) must all founders agree before the company spends or signs?**

*Why does it matter?* Without a limit, one board member can commit the company to any contract. A limit protects everyone, including the person signing.

## 4. Earning your shares

**Over how many years do the founders earn their shares?**

*Why does it matter?* Nobody "gets" their shares on day one. You earn them by staying and working. Investors expect it, and it protects the people who stay if one founder walks away after six months. This is called "reverse vesting".
- ★ Four years (the standard)
- Three years
- Two years

**Is there a "cliff", a first milestone before anything is earned?**

*Why does it matter?* A cliff tests whether the team works together before anyone owns anything. The standard is 12 months. Without a cliff, a founder who leaves after two months still keeps two months of shares.
- ★ Yes: nothing is earned for 12 months, then one big chunk, then month by month
- Yes: nothing for 6 months
- No cliff: shares are earned month by month from day one

**Does the vesting clock start on a different date than the signing date?**

*Why does it matter?* If you have already worked together for a year, you can start the clock earlier so that earlier work counts. Write the date here.

## 5. What happens when someone leaves?

**What if a founder simply quits?**

*Why does it matter?* The Startup Estonia model lets you pick how harshly to treat someone who walks away on their own. "Lose everything" is very harsh and a court may think so too. The middle option is common.
- ★ They keep what they have earned and give up the rest (a "Good Leaver")
- They also give up some of what they earned (a "Voluntary Leaver")
- They lose everything, as if they had done something wrong (a "Bad Leaver")

**If "they also give up some of what they earned": what percentage of the earned shares?**

*Why does it matter?* Typical values are 10 to 50 percent. The percentage is taken from the shares they had already earned.

Your answer: ________  
Suggested: 25

**Who buys the shares of a founder who leaves?**

*Why does it matter?* In Estonia a company can only buy its own shares within strict limits: for example, it can hold shares worth at most one third of its share capital, and only if its net assets stay high enough (Commercial Code § 162). If the company cannot buy, the remaining founders step in.
- ★ The company, which may pass the right on to the remaining founders
- The remaining founders directly

**What if a founder is away for a long time (illness, parental leave, study)?**

*Why does it matter?* The model lets the other founders pause vesting after 56 days of leave in a 12-month period. Pausing vesting during sickness or parental leave can raise discrimination and fairness problems, so our suggestion leaves those out. A lawyer should check your version.
- Nothing special: vesting just keeps running
- ★ If someone is away more than 56 days in a year, the others may pause their vesting, but not for sickness, pregnancy or parental leave
- Same, for any reason at all (the Startup Estonia default)

**How much is the daily penalty if a leaver delays handing over the shares (in euros per day)?**

*Why does it matter?* A penalty only works if it is big enough to matter and small enough to be reasonable. A court can reduce a penalty that is unreasonably high (Law of Obligations Act § 162).
