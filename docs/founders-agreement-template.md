# Founders' Agreement

*Plain-English template for [Company name]*

> **Note:** This is a draft built from the Startup Estonia and EstVCA model documents (Sept 2026) and Estonian law. It is not legal advice and it is not ready to sign. Have a lawyer check it, and make sure the articles of association match it (Schedule C).

## 1. Who are we and what are we building?

> **In plain words:** This agreement is the set of ground rules between the founders and the company. You sign it once, at the start, while you still agree.

**1.1** This agreement is between [Company name] (registry code [registry code]), called "the Company", and the people listed in Schedule A, called "the Founders". It is dated [date].

**1.2** The Company's business, in one sentence: [one sentence describing the business] ("the Business").

**1.3** Each Founder owns the shares shown in Schedule A.

**1.4** This agreement sets the rules between us. It does not replace the Company's articles of association. The articles must say the same things (Schedule C lists what they must say). If the agreement and the articles ever disagree, we follow this agreement between ourselves and change the articles to match.

**1.5** The notes that begin with "In plain words" help you understand each section. They are not part of the rules. If a note and a rule disagree, the rule wins.

## 2. Our jobs and our time

> **In plain words:** Say who does what, and that the company comes first. Anything a founder might do on the side is written down.

**2.1** Each Founder's main job and responsibilities are described in Schedule B.

**2.2** Each Founder works for the Company full-time, which means substantially all of their working time.

**2.3** Other work or business needs the written OK of all the other Founders. Nothing is allowed in advance.

**2.4** If a Founder finds a business chance related to the Business, it goes through the Company, not around it.

**2.5** Deals between the Company and a Founder (or the Founder's family or companies) must be on market terms and in writing.

**2.6** Each Founder owns their shares personally, not through a company.

## 3. How do we decide?

> **In plain words:** Day-to-day business is run by the management board. The big things need a stronger vote. Silence is a "No".

**3.1** All the Founders are members of the management board. Only the shareholders can add or remove members.

**3.2** These matters are for the shareholders to decide. The management board cannot decide them alone:
- Changing the articles of association.
- Issuing new shares, options or convertible loans, or changing the share capital.
- Setting up or changing an option plan, or the size of the option pool.
- The Company buying or selling its own shares.
- Merging, splitting, transforming or closing down the Company.
- Selling all or most of the Company's assets, including its intellectual property, or giving anyone an exclusive licence over all or most of it.
- Paying out profit (dividends).
- Electing or removing members of the management board.
- Approving the annual report and choosing the auditor.
- Deciding what any Founder is paid, and any other deal with a Founder or their family.

**3.3** A big decision passes if it gets at least two-thirds of all votes. The law may ask for more for some decisions (for example, two-thirds of the votes to change the articles, Commercial Code § 175). The higher number always applies.

**3.4** We may vote without a meeting. The management board sends the draft decision to all shareholders in writing (email is fine) with a deadline. Anyone who does not answer by the deadline counts as voting against it (Commercial Code § 173).

**3.5** What a Founder is paid is one of the big decisions listed above.

**3.6** If we cannot decide, any Founder may call a cool-off by writing to the others. For 14 days we talk, with no vote. If we are still stuck, we hire a mediator whom we all accept (or, if we cannot agree on one, one chosen by the Estonian Private Equity and Venture Capital Association or a similar body). The mediator's costs are shared equally. If mediation fails, either side may go to court under section 12.

## 4. Shares, new shares and options

> **In plain words:** Everyone gets first pick when new shares are issued, and nobody can quietly sell or pledge their shares.

**4.1** When the Company issues new shares, options or convertible loans, every shareholder has the right to take their share of them first, in proportion to what they already own (Commercial Code § 193). The shareholders can switch this off for particular shares only by the vote the law requires. We agree to do so only for the option pool and for shares that come from a loan or investment all Founders have approved.

**4.2** We reserve up to 10% of the shares for employees, advisors and board members (the "Option Pool"). The shareholders decide the option plan. Unless the plan says otherwise, options are earned over four years: 25% after one year, the rest month by month over the next three years.

**4.3** No shareholder may pledge or burden their shares without the written OK of all other shareholders.

**4.4** Nobody may receive or buy shares until they have signed the adherence form in Schedule D. That means they accept this agreement.

## 5. Selling shares

> **In plain words:** Founders cannot sell to a stranger without the others getting the first chance to buy.

**5.1** To sell shares to anyone (another shareholder or an outsider), a shareholder needs: (a) written approval from the management board (more than half of the members; the board must approve if the rules of this section are followed); (b) first refusal for the other shareholders under this section; (c) the buyer signing the adherence form in Schedule D; and (d) payment in cash.

**5.2** First refusal: before selling, the seller must offer the shares to the other shareholders, in proportion to what they own, on the same terms and the same price. They have 30 days to say yes. If they do not take all the shares, the seller may sell the rest to the buyer, on terms no better for the buyer than those offered to the other shareholders, within 60 days after that.

**5.3** A Founder may not sell any shares to an outsider while still earning them (see section 6) unless all other Founders agree in writing.

**5.4** These need no approval and no first refusal: moving shares to the Company in the cases covered by section 7.

> **Note:** In Estonia a transfer of shares in an OÜ must be notarised. The articles can allow a written agreement instead, but only if the share capital is at least 10,000 euros and fully paid in, and all shareholders vote for that article (Commercial Code § 149(4) and (6)). The law also gives the other shareholders one month of first refusal on a sale to an outsider unless the articles say otherwise (§ 149(2)).

## 6. Earning your shares (vesting)

> **In plain words:** Nobody owns their shares in full on day one. You earn them by working. If you leave early, the company can take back the part you have not earned yet.

**6.1** The Founders earn their shares over 4 years ("the Vesting Period"). The clock starts on the first day of the month in which this agreement is signed (if signed on days 1 to 15) or on the first day of the next month (if signed later).

**6.2** Nothing is earned during the first 12 months. On the 12-month date, 25% is earned at once. After that, the rest is earned in equal monthly steps until the end of year 4.

**6.3** "Earned" shares are called vested shares. Shares not yet earned are unvested shares.

**6.4** Example: with 4 years and a 12-month cliff, a Founder who leaves after 18 months has earned 37.5% of their shares.

**6.5** If a Founder stops working for the Company before the start date, all their shares count as unvested.

## 7. What happens if someone leaves?

> **In plain words:** This is the part that protects the people who stay. How much of a leaver's shares can be taken back, and at what price, depends on how they left.

**7.1** A Founder "leaves" when they stop working for the Company in any role (employment, management board or service contract). From then on they are a "Former Founder". They stay bound by this agreement, but they are no longer a Founder for the rules about time and roles. The management board updates Schedule A without needing anyone's consent.

**7.2** A "Bad Leaver" is a Founder who leaves in circumstances where there is: 
- a serious breach of their working relationship with the Company that they have not fixed within 30 days after the Company asked them to;
- a conviction for a criminal offence (traffic offences excluded);
- serious damage to the Company that they have not made good within 30 days after being asked;
- within 12 months after leaving, the Company finds out about something that happened before they left that would have made them a Bad Leaver;
- after leaving, a serious breach of the secrecy, non-compete or non-poaching rules in sections 8 and 9

**7.3** The other Founders may decide together that someone is not a Bad Leaver even if one of the above applies.

**7.4** A "Good Leaver" is a Founder who leaves in any way that is not Bad Leaver. This includes quitting on their own, illness, death and being let go without cause. If the Company and the Founder agree on the exit, the Founder is a Good Leaver unless the exit agreement says otherwise.

**7.5** What the Company may take:
- From a Bad Leaver: all shares, vested and unvested, for free. If the Company had already paid for vested shares, the Bad Leaver must repay that money within 30 days after being asked.
- From a Good Leaver: all unvested shares for free, and the vested shares at Fair Value.

**7.6** The Company (or the Founders who remain) must say whether it wants the shares by sending a written notice within 90 days after the Founder became a Leaver. It may take all or part of the shares; taking only part needs the consent of all other Founders. The Leaver then has at least 14 days after the notice (or after Fair Value is set, where the shares are bought at Fair Value) to hand over the shares. The shares may be handed over in several tranches up to a year apart.

**7.7** The Company has the right to acquire the shares. The Company may pass this right on to the Founders who remain (not the Leaver), in proportion to their shares or as they agree in writing. No consent from anyone else is needed for that.

**7.8** By signing, the shareholders approve the Company acquiring its own shares under this section for five years from today, so long as the Company holds no more than the law allows, for a price between 0 euros and the Fair Value (Commercial Code § 162(2)(1)). The law limits the Company to holding shares worth at most one third of the share capital, and the purchase must not reduce the Company's net assets below the share capital and the reserves that cannot be paid out (§ 162(2)). If the Company cannot buy, the remaining Founders buy instead.

**7.9** Fair Value is first set in good faith by the Company. The Leaver has seven days to disagree in writing. If so, an independent expert decides. We pick the expert together within 14 days. If we cannot, the expert is chosen by the management board of the Estonian Private Equity and Venture Capital Association or a similar body in Estonia. If that fails within 14 days, a court chooses. The expert's decision is final.

**7.10** If a Leaver is late in handing over shares, they pay the Company [amount] euros for each day of delay. If the Leaver holds shares through a holding company, both are liable. A court may reduce a penalty that is unreasonably high (Law of Obligations Act § 162).

**7.11** If a Founder is away on leave for more than 56 days in total during any 12 months, the other Founders may pause that Founder's vesting for the extra days. This does not apply to leave because of sickness, pregnancy or parental leave. Paused vesting restarts where it stopped, and the Vesting Period is extended by the same time. The Founder on leave does not vote on this.

## 8. Keeping secrets

> **In plain words:** Customer lists, prices, code, plans and what is said in company meetings stay inside the company.

**8.1** "Confidential Information" means the terms of this agreement and anything about the Company that a reasonable business person would treat as private. This covers finances, strategy, products, prices, customers, suppliers, contracts, code, IT systems, who owns the Company and what is said in meetings. It does not cover information that is already public (unless someone made it public by breaking this agreement), information a person already knew without a duty to keep it secret, or information they received lawfully from someone else.

**8.2** Nobody may use or share Confidential Information except for the Company's business. A person may share it with their lawyers, accountants and other advisors who also have to keep it secret, and where the law or an authority requires it.

**8.3** This lasts while a person is a shareholder and for 3 years after they stop being one. For a Founder who becomes a Former Founder, it lasts as long as they or their holding company hold shares, and for 3 years after that.

## 9. Not competing and not poaching

> **In plain words:** A founder who leaves must not take the idea, the customers or the team to a copycat. The rules are limited in time and place so that they are fair.

**9.1** While a Founder works for the Company, they will not carry on, work for, advise or own a business that competes with the Company's Business in [territory], unless all the other Founders agree in writing. This continues for 12 months after they stop working for the Company, and for as long as they still control the Company plus 12 months after that.

**9.2** Holding shares only as a financial investment is allowed (up to 5% of a listed company), as long as it gives no management role or real influence.

**9.3** During the same period, a Founder will not recruit or persuade the Company's employees, key contractors or board members to leave. Answering a public job advert is not a breach.

**9.4** If a Founder breaks these rules, the Company may ask them to stop at once, hand over any money they earned from the breach, pay [amount] euros for each breach, and pay for any further damage. A breach that continues for a month counts as a new breach.

**9.5** We agree these rules are needed to protect the Company's secrets, customers, team and value. If a court finds any part too wide, it is cut back to what is allowed and the rest stays.

## 10. Who owns what we build?

> **In plain words:** Code, designs, the brand and the data belong to the company, not to the person who happened to write them.

**10.1** Everything a Founder creates for the Company (code, designs, brand, data, know-how) belongs to the Company. Each Founder will sign the Company's IP assignment (the Startup Estonia IP Assignment and License Agreement is a good starting point) for anything made before or after the Company was set up.

**10.2** If a Founder brings existing work or tools into the Company, they list them in Schedule B and state whether they are being given to the Company or only licensed to it.

**10.3** The Company makes sure employees and contractors who build things sign similar IP terms.

## 11. Money

> **In plain words:** Nobody is forced to put in more money. But everyone agrees to help find it.

**11.1** No shareholder has to give the Company a loan, new money or a guarantee. If revenue does not cover costs, the Founders will make reasonable efforts to raise equity or loans.

**11.2** Pay for Founders and dividends are decided as set out in the big decisions in section 3.

**11.3** The Company runs its business in line with good industry practice and the law, and tries to keep its intellectual property safe and not infringe anyone else's.

## 12. Settling disagreements

> **In plain words:** Talk first, then a mediator, then court. Estonian law applies.

**12.1** If we disagree about this agreement, we first meet and try to solve it within 14 days after anyone writes to the others about it.

**12.2** If that fails, we try mediation as described in section 3 before going to court, unless an urgent court order is needed.

**12.3** Estonian law applies to this agreement. Disputes go to the Harju County Court (Harju Maakohus) as the first court.

## 13. Housekeeping

> **In plain words:** The legal small print. Short and standard, but it matters.

**13.1** Each of us confirms that we are free to sign this agreement, that signing it does not break any other agreement or law, and that nobody has applied for our bankruptcy or liquidation.

**13.2** Each Founder says that no spouse's consent is needed for them to sign this agreement. If that changes, they tell the others straight away.

**13.3** This agreement starts when everyone has signed. It lasts until (a) all parties agree in writing to end it, (b) the Company is liquidated, or (c) one person holds all shares. For a person who no longer holds shares, it ends once they have handed over everything they owe, except secrecy, non-compete and non-poaching, and anything owed for a breach before then.

**13.4** Changes need to be in writing and signed by all parties.

**13.5** Each of us is responsible for our own obligations only, not for each other's. The partnership rules of the Law of Obligations Act (§§ 580–618) do not apply to us. We also give up the legal right to cancel or withdraw from this agreement, except as the agreement itself says.

**13.6** Any contractual penalty is meant to push us to perform, not to replace performing. Before claiming a penalty, the Company or the other Founder must give a reasonable time (at most 30 days) to fix the breach, and loses the right if they do not say they will claim it within six months after finding out about the breach.

**13.7** Notices go by email to the addresses on the signature page. Nobody may transfer their rights or duties under this agreement without the others' written consent, except together with their shares in line with this agreement.

**13.8** If a part of this agreement is invalid, we replace it with a valid one that comes as close as possible to what we wanted. This agreement is the whole deal between us on this subject and replaces earlier talks and drafts.

**13.9** This agreement can be signed with qualified electronic signatures (for example with Estonian ID-card or Smart-ID). Some parts (the personal guarantee, the approval for the Company to buy its own shares) need either a handwritten signature or a separate shareholders' decision if the agreement is signed only in "reproducible" form. Ask your lawyer how to sign.

## Schedule C: What must the articles of association say?
- Shareholders have a pro-rata right to take new shares first, and it can be excluded only as set out in this agreement (Commercial Code § 193).
- A transfer of shares needs the approval of the management board (more than half of the members) as a condition for the transfer, and the board must approve if this agreement is followed (§ 149(3)). A transfer without it is void.
- Founders may move all their shares to their own holding company without approval (only if section 2 allows holding companies).
- A shareholder may not pledge or burden a share without the approval of all other shareholders.
- If the share capital is at least 10,000 euros and fully paid in, you may allow a written agreement (instead of a notary) for transferring shares. All shareholders must vote for it (§ 149(6)).
- The list of matters for the shareholders (section 3) and the required vote (at least two-thirds of all votes).
- Notice for shareholder meetings (the model uses seven days) and written votes without a meeting.
- The size of the management board, and that each member may represent the Company.

> **Note:** Startup Estonia publishes model articles (in English and Estonian) that fit its Founders' Agreement. Use them as a starting point. Only the Estonian text of the articles is registered.
